Multi-browser targeting (#615)

Shift-click a panel's navbar to put it in the target set; plain-click a navbar to clear the set and make that panel current. The current panel is always targeted, so with nothing shift-clicked a load behaves exactly as it does today. Dark solid border = current, no aim; blue solid border = the first shift-click, which is both current and the head of the aim; blue dashed outline = aimed at.

Go to annotations navigates to where the loaded 2D features actually are, computed from browser.tracks2D — repeated clicks cycle the chromosome pairs. The fixture's loops are motif-resolution anchors, tens of bp wide, so they draw at the one-pixel floor: small marks on the diagonal, not blocks.

The first shift-click of a new aim also makes that panel current. The load is issued from the current panel, and it is that panel's genome the skips below are measured against — so the aim line names its origin. Aim from the mm10 panel and the hg38 panels are the ones skipped.

Two of the panels are here to exercise the skip paths, which are the parts most likely to be silently wrong: one panel has no map at all, and one is on mm10 while the loads below are hg38. Aim at them and the summary must report them as skipped — not as loaded, and not as an error.

The map buttons broadcast one .hic map to every aimed panel (#680) — through registry.loadHicFileIntoTargets. The rules are not the track rules: a map carries its own genome, so nothing is skipped, the empty panel takes it like any other, and the loads run one after another. A panel whose genome the map replaced is listed under genomeChanged — and any tracks it already had stay drawn over the new genome, which is exactly why that line exists.
